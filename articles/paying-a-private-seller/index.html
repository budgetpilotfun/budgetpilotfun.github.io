Buying & Selling

Paying a private seller without getting burned

Most private-sale advice is written for sellers. This page is for the person holding the money, which is the person with the most to lose if the paperwork turns out to be wrong.

Budget Pilot · September 28, 2026 · 4 min read

A seller who is paid badly has lost money. A buyer who pays badly can lose the money and end up with a car they cannot register, or one that turns out to belong to a lender or to someone else entirely. The pairs below cover the places that happens, in roughly the order you meet them. The left column is the habit to keep, the right is the move that tends to go wrong.

Titling, registration, tax and inspection rules differ by state, so wherever a pair touches paperwork, your state's motor vehicle agency has the final word.

DoDon't

Who is selling

See the title in person.Check the owner's name against the seller's photo ID.
Pay a go-between."It's my brother's car, he's out of town" is a reason to wait.

Only the person named on the title can sign it over. Anyone else is asking you to trust a promise about someone who is not there.

Which car it is

Match the VIN three ways.Title, dashboard plate and the sticker in the driver's door jamb.
Accept a missing title."It's in the mail" or "I'll send it after" means you are buying without proof.

A mismatch can mean a clerical error, a replaced part, or a stolen car. You cannot tell which from the driveway, so treat it as a stop.

Money still owed

Ask directly about a loan.Look for a lienholder named on the title.
Pay the seller in fullwhile a lender still has a claim on the car.

If a loan exists, the lender is paid first and releases the title. The best version is closing the deal at the lender's own branch; the rest is covered in selling a car you still owe on, written from the seller's side.

History

Run the VIN yourselfthrough a vehicle history report you order.
Rely on the seller's printout,however official it looks.

A report you ordered cannot have been edited. Look for salvage or flood brands, odometer oddities and open safety recalls; recalls can be checked for free at nhtsa.gov.

Condition

Pay for an inspectionby a mechanic you chose, before any money changes hands.
Send a depositto hold a car you have not seen.

An inspection usually costs far less than the problem it finds. A seller who refuses one has answered a question you did not need to ask. Deposits for unseen cars are a common listing scam.

The test drive

Ask for a cold startand drive it at highway speed, over rough patches and in stop-and-go.
Arrive to an engine already warmand settle for a lap around the block.

Some problems only show in the first minutes after starting or above a certain speed. A warm engine and a short loop can hide both, whether or not anyone meant them to.

How you pay

Use a traceable methodmade out to the name on the title, ideally at a bank.
Pay by gift card, crypto,or a wire to anyone you have not met.

Payments that cannot be reversed or traced suit only one party, and it is not the buyer. The seller's version of this question is in getting paid safely when you sell privately; the safe options look much the same from either side.

Where you meet

Meet in daylight, in public,or inside a bank lobby.
Carry a large sum of cashto a stranger's address.

Cash is the one payment the seller is sure to like and the one you cannot get back. If you must use it, withdraw it at the bank where you meet.

The price on paper

Write the real priceon a bill of sale with the date, VIN and odometer reading.
Agree to a lower figure"to save on tax."

Understating the price is misrepresenting a sale to the state, and some states assess tax on a book value anyway. It also leaves you with no proof of what you paid if something goes wrong later.

Driving it home

Arrange insurance first,with the VIN, before you take the keys.
Assume the seller's platesor coverage carry over to you.

Whether plates stay with the car or the seller, and whether temporary tags exist, depends on your state. Call your insurer and your motor vehicle agency before the sale, not from the curb.

After the handshake

Title and register promptly,inside your state's deadline.
Leave the signed title in a drawer.

Many states set a window for transferring ownership, and late transfers can carry penalties. Budget for sales or use tax and fees at the counter; our note on registration, title and the other state fees explains what to look up.

The deal itself

Walk away when rushed.There is always another car.
Chase a price far below similar carsthat comes with a story and a deadline.

Deployed owners, estates that must close this week, cars "already with the shipper": the details change, the structure does not. A price that seems too low is usually the bait.

Notice that almost every "do" on this page is slower than its "don't". That is not an accident. The honest version of a private sale takes an extra afternoon, a trip to a bank and a phone call or two, and every shortcut offered to save you that time is worth examining for who it actually saves. A genuine seller loses nothing by waiting for a bank to open or a mechanic to look underneath. The only people who lose by it are the ones who were counting on you not to.
