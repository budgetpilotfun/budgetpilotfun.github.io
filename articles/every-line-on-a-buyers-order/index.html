Buying & Selling

Every line on a buyer's order

The buyer's order is the page where a conversation becomes a sum. Every line on it is either a price, a product, a government charge or a fact about you, and each kind is handled differently.

Budget Pilot · September 28, 2026 · 4 min read

A buyer's order goes by several names: purchase agreement, bill of sale, deal sheet, worksheet. Whatever the dealership calls it, it is the itemized list of what you are agreeing to pay and what you are getting for it, and it usually arrives near the end of a long visit, when attention is lowest. It also comes before the finance contract, so it is the last comfortable place to fix a mistake.

Below is a generic one, reproduced as a table. Real forms differ by dealer and by state, and your order may use other names, combine lines, or include ones not listed here. The last column is the useful part: whether the line is something you can negotiate, something fixed by law or by the facts, or something you simply need to check.

Reading it in the right order

Start at the bottom. The total, the one labeled something like "cash delivered price", should match the out-the-door figure you agreed. If it does not, work upward until you find the line responsible. Most discrepancies turn out to be one of three things: an add-on that was supposed to come off, an optional product that was never discussed, or a trade-in figure that changed between the conversation and the printout.

Then take the lines by type. Anything marked Yes is a matter of agreement, and if it is on the form, you agreed to it or can still disagree. Anything marked No is fixed by law or by a fact, but fixed does not mean correct. Tax computed on the wrong base, or a payoff figure that does not match your lender's quote, are errors, not terms. The Check lines are where a quick look at a VIN plate or a phone screen settles the matter.

The optional products deserve a pause of their own. They can arrive on the order already filled in, sometimes presented as part of the package. Each is a separate purchase you are free to decline, and each has its own arithmetic: we work through service contracts in extended warranty arithmetic and GAP in gap coverage, and when it earns its keep. If you are financing, anything left on the order is usually borrowed, and costs interest for the length of the loan.

Two small habits help. Ask for any blank line or empty box to be struck through or marked zero before you sign, so nothing can be filled in afterward. And if a figure is handwritten or changed by hand, have it initialed and make sure your copy shows the same change. Neither step implies distrust; they simply make the page you keep match the page you signed.

After the buyer's order

If you are borrowing through the dealer, the next document is usually a retail installment contract, and its amount financed should agree with the balance due on this page. Our guide to reading a retail installment contract picks up exactly where this table ends. Ask for a copy of the buyer's order to keep, and take your time with both. Nobody at the desk is in a greater hurry than you allow them to be.
